What to expect from Bay Information Systems

Bay Information Systems is an independent AI consultancy in Cardiff that advises UK organisations on AI they control. It takes no commissions or partner fees from AI vendors, publishes its price (a one-week AI roadmap at a fixed £8,000), and brings in senior associates where an engagement needs more people or specialist skills.

The answers below cover the questions procurement and due diligence usually ask of a supplier, each short enough to paste into an assessment.

Who does the work?

Edward Grundy, the principal, leads every engagement and is the client's point of contact from the first call to handover. He has built and deployed machine learning systems for two decades, for clients including the Bank of England, the Office for National Statistics and Admiral plc.

Where an engagement needs more hands, or a specialism he does not cover, Bay brings in associates: senior engineers and specialists from his network, most of whom he has worked with on previous contracts. Their skills include data engineering and pipelines, natural language processing, computer vision, GPU optimisation and design. Associates work under subcontract to Bay and under the same confidentiality terms as the engagement, and the client knows who they are before they start. Bay remains the contracting party and is responsible for their work.

What happens if the principal is unavailable?

Continuity rests on things the client already holds. Every engagement produces written deliverables (the roadmap, specifications, handover documentation) that another team can pick up. Deployments use open-source software, Marigold and the standard tools around it, on the client's own hardware or in its own cloud account, so nothing stops running and no licence lapses. Deployments that Bay hosts move into the client's account with the same software and no change to the applications that use them (see where the models run). For day-to-day work in progress, an associate who knows the engagement can take over.

How an engagement runs

  1. A first call. Free, about 45 minutes. We complete the private inference checklist or the readiness assessment together, and you keep the results.
  2. The AI roadmap. One week, £8,000 fixed: three interviews with key people, analysis of the data and its constraints, and a written roadmap with a session to walk through it (what a roadmap covers).
  3. Deployment or oversight. A fixed-price deployment quoted from the roadmap, or advisory support while your team or a supplier builds, typically two to three days a week over eight to twelve weeks.
  4. Ongoing advisory. Optional: a monthly retainer of two to four days, with a quarterly review of whether the strategy still holds.
